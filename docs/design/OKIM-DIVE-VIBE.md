# Okim Dive — Vibe & Content Direction

**Status:** Project rule  
**Applies to:** Copy, page content, UX writing, photography direction, illustration, image-generation prompts, and future visual design  
**Brand:** Okim Dive  
**Previous identity:** Okim Explorer — historical source material only

> Every new piece of Okim Dive content should feel like a thoughtful invitation into a beautiful underwater world: learn with care, explore with wonder.

## 1. The Core Feeling

Okim Dive is a beautiful and thoughtful way into the underwater world.

The experience should feel:

- Calm, warm, and welcoming
- Genuinely in love with nature and marine life
- Safe and confidence-building without being fearful
- Curious and quietly adventurous
- Personal rather than corporate
- Visually considered, with an appreciation for beauty and detail
- Open to both local diving and exploration around the world

Okim Dive is **quietly beautiful**, not loudly luxurious; **adventurous**, not extreme; **safety-conscious**, not frightening; **knowledgeable**, not corporate.

## 2. Core Brand Idea

### Primary expression

> **Learn with care. Explore with wonder.**

### Supporting idea

> Okim Dive helps you build confidence in the water, discover the beauty of the underwater world, and keep exploring—one dive at a time.

These lines are directional anchors, not mandatory slogans for every page. Do not repeat them mechanically.

## 3. Content Pillars

Use these four pillars as a filter for future content.

### Love of nature

Show genuine appreciation for:

- Coral reefs and marine life
- Underwater light, colour, and movement
- Islands, coastlines, and natural surroundings
- Quiet moments beneath the surface
- The responsibility that comes with exploring nature

Prefer respect over grand environmental claims. Do not claim that Okim Dive runs conservation programmes or protects the ocean unless that activity is confirmed.

### Safety and confidence

Safety should sound calm, capable, and reassuring.

Use language such as:

- Learn at your own pace
- Build confidence step by step
- Clear guidance
- Focused attention
- Ask questions
- Know what to expect
- Explore with care

Avoid guarantees such as “the safest experience” or “risk-free diving.” Qualify medical, certification, completion, and operational claims.

### Exploration

Present diving as an ongoing journey:

- First underwater experience
- Learning and certification
- Returning to the water
- Practising and building confidence
- Tioman Island diving
- New destinations and international trips

Exploration should feel personal and curious, not extreme or competitive.

### Beauty and thoughtful details

“Pretty” should become a considered appreciation of beauty:

- Reefs and marine life
- Water colour and sunlight
- Island landscapes
- Well-planned experiences
- Small moments worth remembering
- Calm, editorial presentation

Beauty should support the message, not replace it. Do not add decoration, copy, or imagery without a clear purpose.

## 4. Voice and Writing Style

### Sound like

- A knowledgeable local guide
- A patient instructor
- A curious traveller
- Someone who notices beautiful details
- A calm person who helps when things feel uncertain
- Someone excited to share the ocean, not desperate to sell

### Write with

- Plain, polished English
- Friendly confidence
- Short, purposeful sentences
- Specific details where they are verified
- Encouragement without pressure
- Warmth without excessive sentimentality

For Simplified Chinese, preserve the same qualities: natural, polished, concise, warm, and reassuring. Do not translate word-for-word if that creates unnatural phrasing.

### Avoid

- Generic tourism copy
- Loud sales language
- Corporate or institutional phrasing
- Fear-based safety language
- Overly technical explanations when simple language is enough
- Excessive exclamation marks
- Repeated “unforgettable,” “epic,” “luxury,” “world-class,” or “bucket-list” claims
- Poetry so vague that the visitor cannot understand the offer
- Adding paragraphs simply to fill a page

## 5. Copy Decision Test

Before keeping or publishing content, ask:

1. Does this help the visitor understand, decide, prepare, or feel more confident?
2. Does it express love or respect for the underwater world?
3. Does it support safe and responsible exploration?
4. Is the claim verified for current Okim Dive operations?
5. Is this the right page for the message?
6. Is it concise enough to earn its space?

If the answer to these questions is no, remove the content rather than padding the page.

A clear heading does not need supporting copy when the supporting copy only repeats the heading. For example, “Frequently Asked Questions” can stand alone before the questions.

## 6. Page-Level Direction

### Home

Introduce the emotional world of Okim Dive: nature, beauty, learning, confidence, and the possibility of exploring further.

### About

Make the brand and Iris feel personal. Explain the love of the sea and the relationship between learning, practice, and exploration. Do not reduce the story to qualifications or services.

### Courses

Focus on calm confidence, clear guidance, steady progress, and safety. Certification is a beginning, not the end of the visitor’s diving journey.

### Trips

Present Tioman Island and other destinations as part of one continuing dive journey. Explain practical differences such as on-demand versus fixed-date trips only when they help the visitor make a decision.

### Gallery

Let the imagery carry the emotion. Use short captions and context that encourage visitors to look closely rather than making unsupported claims about photography or experiences.

### Contact

Keep the page direct and welcoming. The form is the primary action; alternative contact methods remain secondary.

## 7. Image and Visual Direction

Future images and image prompts should communicate:

- Natural underwater beauty
- Soft ocean blues, sea greens, warm light, and paper-like calm
- Marine life shown with space and respect
- Candid human moments rather than staged adventure poses
- Island landscapes and underwater light
- Thoughtful, editorial composition
- Watercolour atmosphere where illustration is appropriate

Avoid:

- Generic tropical stock-photo energy
- Extreme-sport intensity
- Over-saturated colours
- Fake “underwater paradise” scenes that look disconnected from Okim Dive
- Busy collages and decorative clutter
- Generated artwork presented as verified Okim Dive photography
- Visuals that imply destinations, marine life, facilities, or activities that have not been confirmed

The image should make people want to look closer, not shout for attention.

## 8. Content Truth Rules

- Okim Dive is the current brand. Okim Explorer is historical source material only.
- Do not carry over general hiking, camping, surfing, merchandise, loyalty, equipment-shop, or broad travel-marketplace positioning unless separately approved for Okim Dive.
- Do not invent prices, schedules, availability, payment rules, booking rules, certifications, conservation programmes, accommodation, transport, or facilities.
- Treat supplied PDFs and old documents as source material, not website copy.
- Keep confirmed package details and trip JSON data as the factual source of truth.
- Flag stale, conflicting, or uncertain information before publication.
- Never publish credentials, bank details, passwords, tokens, or other sensitive information.

## 9. Final Standard

A successful Okim Dive page or image should leave the visitor feeling:

> “I would feel cared for here, I would love to see this world, and I can imagine taking the next step.”

If it is beautiful but unclear, revise it. If it is informative but cold, warm it up. If it is exciting but unsafe or exaggerated, qualify it. If it adds no value, remove it.
